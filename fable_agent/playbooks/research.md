# Playbook: Research

Market research, competitive analysis, technical evaluations, and internal
research briefs. The bar: a reader can trust every claim and trace it to a
source.

## Method

1. **Frame first**: write the research questions (3–7, answerable, ranked)
   before gathering anything. Confirm scope: time period, geography,
   segments, exclusions.
2. **Gather with provenance**: for every fact captured, record source name,
   URL/document, publication date, and access date at capture time — not
   retroactively.
3. **Triangulate**: key claims need ≥ 2 independent sources; when sources
   disagree, present the range and say which you weight and why.
4. **Separate fact from inference**: findings state what sources say;
   analysis states what you conclude. Never blend them in one sentence.
5. **Synthesize, don't summarize**: organize by question/theme, not by
   source. A source-by-source walkthrough is a failed synthesis.

## Source hierarchy (prefer higher)

1. Primary data (filings, official statistics, first-party datasets, docs)
2. Peer-reviewed and institutional research
3. Reputable industry analysts and trade press
4. Company marketing and blogs (bias-flagged)
5. Forums/social (color only, never load-bearing)

Date-stamp everything; a 2023 market-size figure presented as current is an
error.

## Brief structure

1. Research questions & scope
2. Key findings (one per question, confidence level: high/medium/low)
3. Detailed findings by theme (evidence, sources inline)
4. Implications & open questions
5. Source list (numbered, dated, linked)

## Honesty rules

- State confidence and gaps explicitly; "no reliable data found" is a valid
  and useful finding.
- Note conflicts of interest in sources (vendor-funded studies, etc.).
- Never fabricate a citation. If a claim can't be sourced, mark it as
  unverified or cut it.
