# Playbook: Business Writing

Proposals, case studies, documentation, memos, and client communications.
The bar: the busiest reader gets the point from the first paragraph.

## Universal rules

- **Bottom line up front**: state the ask, the answer, or the decision in
  the first 2–3 sentences. Background comes after, never before.
- **One idea per paragraph**, topic sentence first. Cut every sentence that
  doesn't inform a decision or action.
- Active voice, concrete verbs, no filler ("in order to" → "to";
  "utilize" → "use"). Plain words over jargon; define necessary terms once.
- Numbers over adjectives: "cut processing time 40% (3.2h → 1.9h)" beats
  "significantly faster".
- Headings and lists for scannability, but prose for reasoning — a document
  that is only bullets explains nothing.

## Document types

**Proposal** (freelance contracts): situation → objectives → scope (in AND
out) → approach & timeline → deliverables → investment (tiered options when
possible) → terms → next step. Price the outcome, itemize the scope.

**Case study**: client context → challenge (quantified) → approach →
results (numbers, before/after) → quote/testimonial → CTA. One page.

**Memo / decision doc**: recommendation first, then options considered with
trade-offs, then supporting analysis, then risks. End with the specific
decision requested and the deadline.

**Documentation**: task-oriented (how to do X), tested steps with expected
outputs, prerequisites stated up front, troubleshooting section for the 3
most likely failures. Update or delete — stale docs are worse than none.

**Client email/update**: subject states the point ("Phase 2 on track —
decision needed on scope by Fri"); 5 sentences max before a bullet
summary; explicit next actions with owners and dates.

## Tone calibration

- Clients: confident, plain, no hedging stacks ("might possibly").
- Executives: shortest version that survives scrutiny; appendix the rest.
- Technical peers: precise, complete, no marketing gloss.

## Quality gate

Read it once as the recipient: Is the ask obvious? Could anything be
misread? Is anything cuttable? Then cut 15%.
